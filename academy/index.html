Our purpose

Depth without pretence, devotion without shortcuts.

Canonical Studies Academy exists to help ordinary believers read Scripture as one book given through many authors: to know where it came from, to read it in its own setting, and to let it shape how they live.

We teach carefully and honestly. Where Christians disagree, we set the positions out fairly. Where scholars are uncertain, we say so. And every hour of study begins and ends in prayer.

How each week works

Five lessons, five ways of reading.

Every week follows the same rhythm, so you always know what the morning holds.

Monday

Teaching

The main teaching of the week, with objectives and the key passages read in full.
Tuesday

Historical context

The people, places, empires and writings behind the text.
Wednesday

Original languages

Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek words studied carefully, without the usual word-study mistakes.
Thursday

Exegesis and the canon

One passage read closely, then traced across the whole Bible.
Friday

Assessment and reflection

A written assignment, review questions and a guided reflection for your private journal.
I

Scripture first

The reading panel shows every assigned passage in full, from the World English Bible, beside the lesson.

II

Your own study space

Highlights, notes and bookmarks on every lesson, a private journal, and flashcards for Hebrew and Greek words.

III

Study your way

Follow the class calendar, catch up if you join late, or switch to self-paced study at any time.

Questions

Before you begin

Is this an accredited qualification?

No. Canonical Studies Academy is independent theological education. Students who complete the programme receive a certificate of completion, which is not a degree, diploma or accredited qualification.

How much time does it take?

About one hour a day, Monday to Friday, for 24 weeks: 120 lessons and 120 scheduled learning hours.

What if I miss a day or join late?

Nothing is lost. Lessons stay open once they are released, and anyone who joins after the start date studies in catch-up mode at their own pace. You can also switch to self-paced study at any time.

Which Bible translation do you use?

The reading panel shows the World English Bible, a modern public-domain translation. You are welcome to read alongside your own Bible.

Do I have to belong to a particular church?

No. The course is taught from a Protestant canon of 66 books and sets out fairly where Catholic and Orthodox Christians differ, and why. Any denominational background you share is private to you and never appears in reports.

Who can see my journal and notes?

Only you. Your private journal and lesson notes are protected in the database so that not even the founder or lecturers can read them.

How are lessons written?

Lesson drafts may be prepared with the help of AI tools. Every lesson is reviewed by the founder before students can read it, and Scripture text is always taken from the published World English Bible, never generated.

What do I need?

A phone, tablet or computer with a browser, and an email address to sign in.
